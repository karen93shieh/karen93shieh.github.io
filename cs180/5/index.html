Part 1 — Sampling Loops & Diffusion Tasks

In Part 1, I implement the forward diffusion process, one-step and iterative denoising, and then use these routines for generation, classifier-free guidance, image-to-image translation, inpainting, visual anagrams, and hybrid images.

1.1 — Implementing the Forward Process

The forward process adds noise to a clean image x₀ according to the schedule ᾱₜ:

xₜ = √ᾱₜ · x₀ + √(1 − ᾱₜ) · ε, where ε ~ N(0, I).

Using the provided alphas_cumprod, I implemented forward(im, t) to generate noisy versions of the Campanile at various timesteps, and visualized the increasing noise for t ∈ {250, 500, 750}.

1.2 — Classical Denoising (Gaussian Blur)

I attempted to denoise the noisy Campanile images using classical Gaussian blur filtering via torchvision.transforms.functional.gaussian_blur. The goal is to see how far a simple low-pass filter can go on its own.

Deliverables

  • For each noisy Campanile image (t = 250, 500, 750), show the best Gaussian-denoised result side-by-side.
  • Reflection: Gaussian blur struggles significantly compared to diffusion-based denoisers because it uniformly smooths the entire image, losing important high-frequency details and edges. It cannot distinguish between noise and actual image content, whereas diffusion models learn the natural image manifold and can intelligently restore structure.

1.3 — One-Step Denoising with DeepFloyd IF

Using stage_1.unet and the prompt embedding for a high quality photo, I performed a single denoising step on noisy Campanile images. The UNet predicts the noise (first 3 channels of the output), which I then used with the forward equation to estimate x₀.

Deliverables

  • One-step denoised Campanile for t = 250, 500, 750.
  • Discussion: As the noise level increases, the one-step denoising quality degrades significantly. At t=250, the model can recover reasonable structure, but at t=750, a single denoising step struggles to recover fine details and the image appears much blurrier. This demonstrates why iterative denoising is necessary for high-quality results.

1.6 — Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)

I extended the iterative denoiser to compute both unconditional and conditional noise estimates and combined them using

ε = εᵤ + γ · (ε꜀ − εᵤ), with guidance scale γ = 7.

For unconditional guidance, I used the empty prompt "". The conditional embedding corresponds to a high quality photo.

Deliverables

  • Implementation of iterative_denoise_cfg completed in Colab notebook.
  • 5 CFG-sampled images for a high quality photo with γ = 7.
  • Comparison: CFG samples show significantly improved sharpness, detail, and more coherent structure compared to non-CFG samples. The guidance helps the model produce images that better adhere to the prompt.
